# Admission And Signing Guidance

**DRAFT PLACEHOLDER - NOT APPROVED FOR PRODUCTION**

Version: `draft-2026-08-02`

## Purpose

This working note preserves admission and consent scenarios that the enrolment workflow
must handle. It is not legal advice and must be reviewed against current Victorian and
Commonwealth requirements before Rosewood relies on it.

## Signatures And Authority To Consider

The final approved guidance must state who is permitted or required to complete and
sign each stage. Scenarios requiring an explicit decision include:

- a student over 15 who is living independently
- a parent as understood under the `Family Law Act 1975 (Cth)`
- parents who are separated, including whether both signatures are required
- a current court or parenting order that affects contact, responsibility, information
  sharing, decision-making or the relationship between the family and school
- an informal, kinship or other carer who has day-to-day care and with whom the student
  regularly lives
- a secondary student completing relevant sections and co-signing

## Working Notes Requiring Legal Approval

The source process used for workflow research included guidance that, in the absence of
a current court order, parents of a child under 18 may have parental responsibility; it
also requested both parents' signatures for separated parents or a copy of an order
that affected the school relationship. Rosewood must not adopt those propositions as a
blanket rule without legal review and an approved exception/escalation procedure.

For an informal carer, the source process contemplated a statutory declaration,
day-to-day care, the student regularly living with the carer and the carer giving other
day-to-day consent such as for excursions. It also described statutory declarations as
lasting 12 months and a legally responsible parent's wishes prevailing in a dispute.
Rosewood must confirm the current legal position, evidence, duration, dispute and child
safety rules before using that model.

## Workflow Controls

- Record legal responsibility, permission to contact and required-signatory status as
  separate concepts.
- Do not infer authority from who entered the expression of interest, paid fees, lives
  at an address or is listed as the primary contact.
- Ask families to identify relevant orders without requiring sensitive details in an
  ordinary email.
- Route uncertainty, disputes and restricted contact to an authorised staff member.
- Freeze one application revision and require each approved signer to verify and sign
  independently.
- Allow an approved override only with a reason, decision-maker, timestamp and evidence
  reference.
